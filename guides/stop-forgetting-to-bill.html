Stop forgetting to bill customers: a simple weekly routine

You did the work. You just never sent the bill, or never followed up on it. Here's a routine that takes about 15 minutes a week and catches almost all of it.

Step 1: One list of open jobs

Not three notebooks and a text thread — one list. A spreadsheet is fine (here's how to set one up). Every job goes on it the day it's agreed.

Step 2: Bill the same day you finish

The longer a finished job sits, the more likely it is to be forgotten — and the slower customers pay. If you can't send the invoice on the spot, mark the job Done so it shows up in your weekly check.

Step 3: Pick one time each week

Monday morning, Friday afternoon, whatever you'll stick to. Put it in your phone as a repeating reminder. In that 15 minutes:

  • Send an invoice for everything marked Done.
  • Text or call anyone whose invoice is more than 2 weeks old.
  • Check for quotes you never heard back on and send a one-line follow-up.

Step 4: Make the follow-up easy to say

Most people aren't refusing to pay — they forgot too. A short, friendly message works:

"Hi, it's [name] with [business]. Just a reminder the invoice for [job] on [date] is still open — $[amount]. You can pay by [your payment options]. Thanks!"

Step 5: Take deposits on bigger jobs

For larger jobs, asking for part of the price up front protects your cash and means fewer bills to chase later. Customers expect it more than you'd think.
